Split-LR fix + resume-from-peak validation for the R2-Dreamer collapse · 2026-06-30

The Question

R2-Dreamer drone-racer trains to a ~40% lap peak then collapses (critic_loss rises, value destabilizes; one run's critic diverged to 502 terminally). Three optimizers share a single lr=1e-4; reference impls split (wm 1e-4, actor-critic 3e-5). Runs are nondeterministic (~5 days each). Proposed: (1) split the LR to 3e-5 on actor/critic; (2) build policy-based replay refill and resume the banked 40% checkpoint with split LR to test in hours whether it holds; (3) late entropy anneal if saturation persists. How effective?

Bottom line

The council endorses splitting the LR (cheap, free, reference-validated) but rejects the resume-from-peak experiment as designed — it cannot produce a readable signal. The unanimous miss caught in peer review: the answer is already in the banked TensorBoard logs; diagnose before spending GPU.

Where the Council Agrees

  • Split LR is worth doing but treats a symptom. 10-min change, numbers from DreamerV3/SkyDreamer. But lowering actor/critic LR slows divergence, it doesn't remove the mechanism — expect later collapses, not no collapses. (Contrarian, First-Principles, Outsider, Executor)
  • The resume-from-peak test is broken. Collapses arrive 29–36M steps after peak; "holds for hours" (<1M steps) proves nothing against a slow-onset failure. Policy-refill warm-starts the good policy → survivorship bias → false positive. (Contrarian, Executor)
  • Reproducibility first. Unseeded + nondeterministic = any single result is noise; you cannot attribute a recovery to the LR. Seed the env before changing one variable. (Executor, Contrarian)
  • Skip the entropy anneal for now. Saturation moved in only 1 of 3 collapses — a symptom, not the driver. One variable at a time. (First-Principles, Outsider, Contrarian)

Where the Council Clashes

Is collapse one mechanism or two? The Contrarian reads the data as two diseases: monotonic critic blow-up (the 502 run = genuinely too-hot optimizer) vs. recoverable crater (critic chasing a target the bang-bang actor moved). One LR knob can't fix both. The First-Principles thinker counters: it's one root — a nonstationary value target — and the fix isn't LR alone but the DreamerV3 stabilizers (return normalization, slow-target critic, free-bit KL). Both can be right: LR is necessary, not sufficient.

How optimistic to be? The Expansionist puts ~70% on the resume holding and reframes collapse as a publishable "research asset." Every other advisor — and all five peer reviewers — rejected this as the biggest blind spot: it sells iteration-economy upside while the test it relies on can't be read.

Blind Spots the Peer Review Caught

  • The answer is already banked — for free. All five reviewers independently landed here: pull the existing TB curves at the 8M / 29M / 36M collapse onsets and overlay world-model / repr / KL loss and imagined value-target vs. realized return. If WM/repr drift leads the critic, the LR is rearranging deck chairs. Zero GPU. Decides the whole question.
  • Are the DreamerV3 critic stabilizers even present? No-image-recon R2-Dreamer's critic regresses on imagined rollouts; without a slow-target critic + return EMA, the value target is nonstationary by construction. Verify these exist before touching LR.
  • The replay buffer is the hidden confound. Unchecckpointed episode-level replay near the peak is stale/off-distribution — a plausible collapse driver independent of LR, and it contaminates any resume test regardless of seeding.

The Recommendation

Verdict

Do not launch the resume-from-peak run as designed — it's an unreadable test. Instead, in order: (1) mine the banked TB logs at the three collapse onsets (free, today) — overlay wm/repr/KL loss + value-target-vs-realized-return + saturation; determine whether representation drift leads the critic and whether it's one mechanism or two. (2) Confirm the slow-target critic + return-EMA are wired. (3) Ship split-LR and a global seed together (≈2 hrs). (4) Launch one fresh seeded split-LR run. Keep the resume experiment only as a same-seed A/B after a fresh run collapses. Realistic effect of split-LR alone: delays the collapse, ~30–40% chance it removes it; the log-diagnosis is what tells you if you're even turning the right knob.

Full advisor responses

Contrarian Misreading the evidence; the test hands a false positive

The split-LR fix is plausible but you are misreading the evidence, and the validation design has a hole that will hand you a false positive.

The data does not say "critic LR too high." It says critic_loss rises at collapse (1.27→1.79, 0.69→0.86). A too-hot critic diverges monotonically (your 8M run: value→502). Your two recoverable craters show critic loss rising then recovering — the critic chasing a target that moved, not an optimizer blowing up. The mover is the actor/world-model: bang-bang policy (sat 0.18→0.30) shifts the state distribution, value targets jump (60→2.7 is a collapse in returns, i.e. the policy got worse first), critic loss rises tracking it. Lowering critic LR makes it track the moving target slower, which can deepen, not prevent, the crater. And baseline-64px collapsed with NO saturation change and a HIGHER value — different mechanism, same LR. One fix, two mechanisms: at least one won't respond.

The validation design is the real flaw. You resume the lucky 40% peak and watch "in hours." But collapses arrive at 29M and 36M — tens of millions of steps after peak. Holding for hours proves nothing; the failure is slow-onset. You'll declare victory and eat the same collapse five days later. Worse: policy-refill warm-starts the buffer with the GOOD policy — survivorship bias baked into the test. Run controls: split-LR AND critic-only-LR AND a third seed.

First Principles Right instinct, wrong frame — you dropped the stabilizers

Three optimizers, one shared symptom: critic_loss elevated and spiking right at every collapse. That's not three bugs, it's one — the critic is chasing a target it cannot pin down.

Dropping actor+critic to 3e-5 slows the divergence, it doesn't remove the mechanism. DreamerV3 doesn't pair the 3e-5 with a lower LR alone — it ships percentile return normalization, critic EMA/slow-target regularization, and free-bit KL specifically because the value target is nonstationary. You bolted on a single LR and inherited the instability the rest of the recipe was designed to suppress. The real question: is your critic regularized like DreamerV3's, or did you port the LR and drop the stabilizers? Check ReturnEMA and whether you have a slow/target critic before touching LR.

The resume-and-test idea is genuinely smart and the actual experiment that matters. A 5-day blind run where "early/late/oscillate is luck" is a slot machine, not an experiment. Policy-based replay refill turning a 5-day question into a 3-hour one is the highest-leverage thing on the list — do it first. But know what resume-from-peak tests: holding a banked peak is easier than reaching it. A split-LR resume that holds 40% proves stability, not that you'd ever climb there from scratch. Save the entropy-floor anneal — saturation appeared in only one of three collapses. One variable at a time.

Outsider Why does a converged policy spontaneously fall apart?

The split LR will help at the margin, but everyone here is debugging the symptom and skipping the obvious question: why does a CONVERGED policy that completes 40% of laps spontaneously fall apart? Stable RL doesn't do that. Lowering LR to 3e-5 slows divergence — you'll likely buy yourself later collapses, and wait 5 days to find out.

The unexamined assumption: that the collapse originates in the OPTIMIZER. Three failure modes (critic loss creeping up, critic exploding to 502, saturation jumping) treated as one knob. The saturation signal — run one jumped 0.18→0.30, run two stayed flat at 0.10. That's two DIFFERENT diseases. A single LR change can't fix both.

And nobody's questioning this: your environment is unseeded and nondeterministic, and you've concluded collapse-timing is "luck." But if identical configs diverge into qualitatively different failures, that's not bad luck — that's a system on a knife's edge where tiny perturbations decide the outcome. That points to a feedback loop (world-model drift → bad imagined returns → critic chases them → actor exploits model error → worse data → worse model), not an LR 3× too high. Before burning 5 days: log per-update what the critic is actually regressing onto. Is the value target diverging from realized return? Test the resume plan first — it's cheap — but don't pre-commit to "LR was the problem."

Executor Ship split-LR; the resume shortcut is a trap — seed first

Three optimizers already exist. Splitting LR is a 10-minute change. Do it — free, and DreamerV3/ SkyDreamer already validated those exact numbers.

The resume-validation shortcut is the trap. agent_best.pt is a known-lucky peak that already collapsed once. Even with a perfect refill, you cannot distinguish "split LR held the peak" from "this resume got lucky" — runs are non-reproducible by your own admission. A resume that holds 3 hours tells you nothing; one that collapses tells you nothing either (baseline collapsed at 36M, not at resume time). Net-new code to buy a signal you can't read. Motion, not progress.

Monday: (1) ship split-LR (10 min). (2) Before anything else, spend 2 hours adding a global seed + torch.use_deterministic_algorithms and seeding the env — you'll never debug oscillatory collapse without reproducibility; highest-leverage 2 hours on the board. (3) Kick off ONE fresh seeded run with split LR. (4) Build the policy-refill in parallel, but only spend it on a resume after the fresh run also collapses, as an A/B on the same seed. Split-LR + seed is the cheap, readable test.

Expansionist You've stumbled onto a research asset ⚑ flagged: biggest blind spot

Split the LR and you'll buy stability — but you're treating this like a bug fix when you've stumbled onto a research asset. Your critic_loss spikes + value blowups are a measurable, repeatable instability signature. Three bigger plays the plan undersells:

(1) Checkpoint the replay buffer and you unlock the entire iteration economy — persisted replay turns every run into a warm-startable branch point. ~6 experiments/month to dozens. The LR-split isn't the win; the resumability is.
(2) Collapse is a curriculum signal — runs collapse near the same return ceiling because the policy outgrows the value function's calibration range (value 60→150→502). Treat the collapse boundary as where to inject difficulty. A free "ready for harder" detector.
(3) Determinism is upside — seed env + replay and the oscillations become ablatable, collapse→recovery a publishable phenomenon. A 40%-lap policy on one 4090 from-scratch is already strong.

Expected effectiveness: split-LR + resume holds the peak ~70% likely. But the real ROI is the iteration loop you're building, not this one save.
